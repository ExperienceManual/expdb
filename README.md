# Experience Database

**Under construction**

These files are an attempt to define the range of human experiences that have
names and how they relate to each other.

What you find in most dictionaries about these experiences is pretty thin and
sometimes circular. In everyday conversation, we sometimes use overlapping
concepts as synonyms when they're not.

The goal is to have a well-curated and community-owned database of 
experiences that can be communicated in language. These
sensations and impulses consume our lives, but the words
and concepts we have for them are surface froth. We hope that a
more comprehensive and disciplined framework can become a valued
psycho-technology that points towards inner motivations that don't yet have words.

Each culture, and perhaps each person, may have a slightly
different understanding of these experiences. You can open an issue
or pull request with corrections or suggestions. Porting the collection to
other languages is also welcome.

# Details

One aim of this collection is to better organize an extremely complicated
phenomenon without overwhelming. This is not a book meant to be consumed in a linear way. It is a hypertext reference and this section documents the structure in use.
Does it perfectly capture the infinite nuances of human experience? Of course not.
Is it an advancement over a dictionary? That is for you to decide.

## Emotions vs Experiences

We purposefully avoid the word "emotion":

- People don't agree on what "emotions" really are
- The word "emotion" can carry cultural baggage
- The common understanding of "emotion" doesn't begin to cover all the experiences we have. For example, is "curiosity" an emotion? How about "introspection"?

The academic study of "emotions" (experiences) seems to fall into three camps:

1. A search for the "basic emotions." There's a hypothesis that complex emotions may consist of basic components like fear, joy, disgust, love, etc.
1. A focus on the neurobiological aspects of experience, like brain structure and neurotransmitters.
1. The view that emotions are "constructed" and culturally dependent.

All of this work is useful and illuminating. We would welcome professional
emotion researchers to comment on the entries for their specialty area.
We will certainly consider linking to well-established scientific resources.

## Core Philosophies

Each JSON file here describes an experience as being like a single 'letter' in the 'alphabet' of our inner world. They can be combined with others to form 'words', which are then strung together to form 'sentences' and ultimately the crazy autobiography we each live inside. For example, you can probably imagine how it might be possible to feel "fear/anger/guilt" that dissipates into "admiration/humor/relief/shame".

It took hundreds of thousands of years for our species to figure out how to represent spoken language as marks pressed in clay or scratched onto bones. It took longer still for music to be written down. Everyone understands that these abstract notations are directional – only useful once the amazing brain translates the marks to something you've experienced. The letter "A" is arbitrary and flexible, but we're all used to dealing with it in large collections of letters. So it is with these experiences.

Here are some guiding assumptions:

- It's not easy to introspect these experiences, but neither is it easy to learn what sounds letters make. It takes years of practice for children to learn to read.
- We must incorporate established science where possible, including psychology, neuroscience, primatology, emotion research, meditation research, philosophy, etc.
- Our inside experience is related to the neurotransmitter chemicals in the brain, but it is not the same.
- Humans are profoundly social creatures with complex mental models of social interactions. A given experience feels very different from the inside than when observed in someone else. All of these files are from a first-person perspective, though some entries may reference what the "person of interest" may be experiencing. For example, the file for "hubris" assumes that the person in question is probably feeling "self-confidence".
- One of society's functions is to decide which experiences to encourage and which to discourage. This collection must include experiences that people might consider harmful or socially inappropriate.
- We need to allow for multiple underlying explanations for experiences as long as they're coherent and illuminating.
- We need to deal carefully with mental health problems. This collection is not a self-help replacement for the DSM and professional therapy.

## Experiences from Different Angles

Distinctions between some experiences can be subtle, so these files try to shed light on the topic in several ways:

- Description - defines the experience and its sources
- Perceptions - things that you may be perceiving as part of this experience
- Desires - underlying motivations that may play a role in this experience
- Sensations - other experience fragments that may play a role in this overall experience. For example "anxiety" and "delight" are components of many more complex experiences
- Results - a likely outcome of the experience
- Quote - what someone might say who's having this experience
- Examples - situations where this experience may happen
- Variations - many experiences have different flavors
- Comparisons - highlight differences and similarities of closely related experiences
- Commentaries - quotations chosen to highlight various aspects of the experience
- Spectrum - some experiences have different words for varying strength of feeling.
- Model - A section reserved for a technical description of the experience.

## Terminology

Some word usage may warrant elaboration: 

- Agent/Agency - this is a general term for a complex system acts upon the world stage. It can be applied to a single person/creature or a group of them. For example a group of people might also express their agency.
- Environment - we use this term in the most general sense of "surroundings", not just the natural environment. I.e. classrooms and offices are environments just as much as a rainforest.
- Dismay - this term is used as the opposite of "delight". Some people may find "dismay" to be too strong, but other alternatives like "disappointment" seem imperfect as well.
- Pure/Purity - this just means anything that is close to 100%. It can apply to anything we want to be spotless, whether that's your reputation, your health, your amorous activities, or anything else.

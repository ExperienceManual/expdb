These files are an attempt to define the range of nameable human experiences 
names and how they relate to each other. What you find in most dictionaries about these experiences is pretty thin and sometimes circular. In everyday conversation, we sometimes use overlapping concepts as synonyms when they're not.

We humans understand that sensations and impulses can consume our lives, but the concepts and words we have for them are sometimes inadequate. The goal of the Experience Manual (EM) is to have a well-curated and community-owned database of human experiences that can be communicated with language. 

We hope to become a comprehensive and disciplined framework that may in turn become a valued tool.

Each culture, and perhaps each person, may have a slightly different understanding of these experiences. You can open an issue or pull request with corrections or suggestions. Porting the collection to other languages is also welcome.

However, it's worth noting that this library comes from a scientific, positive, and holistic perspective:

- Rationality is a valuable tool.
- There are 'meta-truths' that are useful in understanding provincial truths.
- The future of human civilization needs alchemical psychology to start catching up with physical chemistry.
- Understanding a few slices of experience is not enough. Either everything connects or it all falls apart. We need a 'periodic table' of experience, not just gold and oxygen.
- It is possible to say something about human experience beyond "it is what it is."

# Details

One aim of this collection is to better organize an extremely complicated
phenomenon without overwhelming. This is not a body of data meant to be consumed in a linear way. It is intended as a hypertext reference, and this README documents the structure.
Do these files perfectly capture the infinite nuances of human experience? Of course not.
Are they an advancement over a dictionary? That is for you to decide.

## Emotions vs Experiences

We purposefully avoid the word "emotion":

- People don't agree on what "emotions" really are.
- The word "emotion" carries cultural baggage.
- The common understanding of "emotion" doesn't begin to cover all the experiences we have. For example, is "curiosity" an emotion? How about "introspection"?

The academic study of "emotions" (experiences) seems to fall into three camps:

1. A search for the "basic emotions." There's a hypothesis that complex emotions may consist of basic components like fear, joy, disgust, and love.
1. A focus on the neurobiological aspects of experience, like brain structure and neurotransmitters.
1. The view that emotions are "constructed" and culturally dependent.

All of this work is useful and illuminating in some way. We welcome professional
emotion researchers to comment on the entries for their specialty area.
We will certainly consider linking to well-established scientific resources.

But instead of just 'emotions', this project focuses on the broader experience of creatures living in a huge world. 'Experience' is defined as "what it feels like when the brain or body has some kind of stimulation: a sensation, thought, feeling, desire, whatever." Experiences are often complex and multilevel, occurring across multiple parts of the brain, heart, gut, and body.

### Experience, Awareness, Beliefs

The many experiences happening in the mind and body are often subtle and confusing.
It's almost always too much to process carefully, so the brain selects a subset of experiences to focus on. This subset is 'awareness'.

For example, as you're reading this right now, your body is feeling many things: pressure on your back or feet, hunger/fullness, temperature, etc. You may have anxiety about a family situation or an upcoming event. Most of the time you tune these things out to focus on the task at hand, but they affect your mood, patience, ability to focus, and perhaps your perception of these words.

We can become aware of most sensations if we want to. But there are things that shape our perceptions that are quite difficult to become aware of: beliefs, assumptions, desires, and biological biases all bend perception. We're simply not built to see their impact. You know of their existence mainly when they become so obvious that you can't ignore them.

### Summary

- The world and your desires cause your nervous system to 'wiggle' in a complicated way.
- How these crazy dissonant stimulations feel from the inside is the 'experience'.
- 'Awareness' is the slice of the overall experience that you're currently focusing on.
- Awareness is flavored by:
    - Fragments of experience you're ignoring or blocking.
    - Assumptions and beliefs that lens your perception.

## Core Philosophies

In the book of a human life, a single experience is like a single 'letter' in the 'alphabet' of our inner world. They can be combined with others to form 'words', which are then strung together to form 'sentences' and ultimately the autobiography we each live inside. For example, you can probably imagine how it might be possible to feel "fear/anger/guilt" that dissipates into "admiration/humor/relief/shame".

It took thousands of years for our species to figure out how to represent spoken language as marks pressed in clay or scratched onto bones. It took longer still for music to be written down. Everyone understands that these abstract notations are directional – only useful once the brain translates the marks into something you've experienced. The letter "A" is arbitrary and flexible, but we're all used to dealing with it. So it is with these JSON files.

Here are some guiding assumptions:

- **Invitation for introspection** - This collection is not for preaching. For example, we won't ever suggest that the reader should "Treat people nicely." Instead, "You might consider whether treating others better will help yourself or the general environment." It is not our place to moralize things.
- **Don't over-complicate** - We incorporate established science where possible, including psychology, neuroscience, primatology, emotion research, meditation research, philosophy, etc. There is a library's worth of valid research that needs to be synthesized into something more digestible.
- **Don't over-simplify** - It's not easy to introspect these experiences, but neither is it easy to learn what sounds letters make. People of the future will forgive us if we're earnest about this balance.
- **Brain influences mind** - Our inside experience is related to the neurotransmitter chemicals in the brain, but it is not the same.
- **First-person perspective** - A given experience feels very different from the inside than when observed in someone else. Most of these files are from a first-person perspective, though some entries reference what the "person of interest" may be experiencing. For example, the entry for "hubris" assumes that the person in question is probably feeling "self-confidence".
- **Go where the people are**
    - **Accessible language** - The terminology used should be reasonably common. There are some beautifully precise words in the underlying fields that don't make much sense to regular people. Here's a non-exhaustive list of words that only professionals like: "cognitive", "epistemology", "ontology", "reaction formation".
    - **Share opposing views** - Commentaries can take provincial perspectives, but the main text should strive to explain what actually is, not what should be.
    - **Allow what is/was** - History shows that human experience commonly includes: the supernatural, mating, violence, unfairness, and other things that trigger people. Referring to these things can be done maturely.
    - **Be welcoming** - People are here because they're curious; there's no need to turn them away with abrasive opinions or by assuming they have to believe X. For instance, the scientific worldview is that evolution is a natural fact and that it doesn't conflict with the beauty of the world's religions. However, many people see otherwise. EM cannot solve this debate, and there's no need to take sides.
- **Thousand year rule** - we won't take a specific perspective other than to take the long view:  encourage results that our descendants 1,000 years in the future might care about. We need to let society decide which specific experiences to encourage and which to discourage.
- **No Center** - The mind is a complicated emergent system with no single component or concern at the center. No particular part of the mind (ego, soul, amygdala, etc) is a keystone.
- **Architect for multiple perspectives** - We need to allow for multiple underlying explanations for experiences as long as they're coherent and illuminating.
- **Global** - The initial collection is in English, so it's expected that commentaries and perspectives will be centered on the English-speaking world, but the goal is to cover as much of the human condition as feasible.
- **Living Manual** -We should strive to be a hub for reputable and well-considered resources relating to these topics.
- **Be responsible** - We need to deal carefully with mental health problems. This collection is not a self-help replacement for the DSM and professional therapy.
- **Don't forget to smile** - Yes, we should do our best, but the future will smile at our ignorance no matter what. Our job is to inch forward and be well-adjusted people.

## Experiences from Different Angles

Distinctions between experiences can be subtle, so these files try to shed light on the topic in several ways. These are the fields that may be present in each file:

- Description - defines the experience in an 'elevator pitch' sentence or two.
- Perceptions - a series of things that you may perceive as part of this experience. This is meant to be a more 'technical' set of conditions that can lead to the experience.
- Desires/Impulses - underlying motivations that may play a role in this experience.
- Sensations - other experience fragments that may play a role in this overall experience. For example, "anxiety" and "delight" are components of many complex experiences.
- Results - likely outcomes of the experience.
- Quote - what someone might say who's having this experience
- Examples - situations where this experience may be felt
- Variations - different flavors of the experience. For example, most social experiences have both first-person and second-person versions. 'Awkwardness' feels different when it's you that's awkward versus when it's someone else displaying awkwardness.
- Comparisons - highlight differences and similarities of closely related experiences.
- Commentaries - quotations chosen to highlight various aspects of the experience.
- Spectrum - some experiences have different words for varying strength of feeling. These are basically synonyms for stronger and weaker forms of the same underlying experience.
- Model - A technical description of the experience. See [LINK]

## Terminology

- **Experience** - what it feels like on the inside of brain and body activity. Includes Sensations, Desires, Agency, and Patterns.
- **Outcome** - an event/state that happens in the branching flow of events/states as the environment changes.
- **Perception** - an outcome of your mental processing that you become aware of.
- **Sensation** - a perception that something has happened *to* you.
- **Desire/Impulse** - a goal or preference to reach a particular outcome.
- **Agent** - a complex system that acts upon the world stage. It can be a single person/creature or a group of them.
- **Agency** - the sense that an Agent has the ability to choose how they interact with the environment. The 'free-will' debate is not relevant because the vast majority of people live in a mental world where they make their own choices.
- **Patterns** - a human brain is the most amazing pattern-matching-prediction system in the known universe. It's exposed to a flood of input and noise and has the impossible job of making sense of an infinitely complex world. It does this by creating fuzzy-matching structures, triggered by input, that link to predictions about what could come next and how various actions could influence outcomes. EM definitions may refer to "patterns", which include the matching conditions as well as the resulting predictions. For example, you might have a pattern that suggests when there's a giant pyramid-shaped object, what comes next might have something to do with ancient Egypt, even if it's made of cheese rather than stone.
- **Prediction** - a recognition that a particular outcome is possible, but without feeling particularly invested in whether that outcome happens or not.
- **Expectation** - a recognition that a particular outcome is very likely, often with a desire for the outcome to happen or not happen.
- **Assumption** - a prediction or expectation about the world that's nearly invisible until broken.
- **Environment** - we use this term in the most general sense of "surroundings", not just the natural environment. I.e., homes, classrooms, and offices are environments just as much as a rainforest.
- **Dismay** - this term is used as the opposite of "delight". Some people may find "dismay" too strong, but other alternatives, like "disappointment," seem imperfect as well.
- **Pure/Purity** - this just means anything that is close to 100%. It can apply to anything we want to be spotless, whether that's your reputation, your health, your amorous activities, or anything else. 
- **Existential/Existential Self** - this term is used to represent the idealized part of yourself that is concerned about its relationship to the Universe. Many people call this "soul" or "spirit". Whether or not we ever find a "soul" in an FMRI scan is irrelevant -- history shows that the human brain gives special deference to its relationship to Existence.
- **Inner model** - everything and everyone important in our life gets associated with a collection of many patterns. You know what's likely to come next when your spouse gets 'that look' or has a bad day at work. And it's not just people – you have patterns of expectations for your car (the seats get hot in the summer), your job (the end-of-quarter crunch), and everything else. The collection of all the patterns surrounding a given topic is your 'inner model' of that person or thing.
- **Truth** - Turns out 'truth' is a [tough concept](https://iep.utm.edu/truth/). We focus only on 'subjective truth' since that's what the human brain actually deals with. While we hope there is such a thing as 'objective truth', it's sadly not all that relevant when dealing with people. EM defines 'subjective truth' as a "pattern that's reliable enough that you're willing to base important decisions on it."
- **Consciousness** - the experiences and desires that you can access and describe to others.
- **Subconscious** - experiences and desires that are difficult to perceive, but can be accessed with therapy, self-honesty, meditation, prayer, and/or diligence.
- **Unconscious** - internal experiences and desires that cannot be accessed directly. Their existence must be implied.
- **Virtue** - a characteristic of a person viewed as positive. e.g. "courage", "wisdom", etc. Note that a virtue in one subculture (e.g. 'compassion' or 'tough love') might not be viewed the same in another.
- **Moral Rule** - A deeply important social rule established by the society you live in.

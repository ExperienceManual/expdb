# Experience Database

**Under Construction**

These files are an attempt to define the range of human experiences that have
names and how they relate to each other.

What you find in most dictionaries about these experiences is pretty thin and
sometimes circular. In everyday conversation, we sometimes use overlapping
concepts as synonyms when they're not.

The goal is to have a well-curated and community-owned database of 
experiences that can be communicated with language. At some level, these
emotions and impulses consume our lives, but at another level, the words
and concepts we have for them are surface froth. We hope that a
more comprehensive and disciplined framework can become a valued
psychotechnology that points towards inner motivations that don't yet have words.

It is recognized that each culture, and perhaps each person, may have a slightly
different understanding of these experiences. You can open an issue
or pull request with corrections or suggestions. Porting the collection to
other languages is also welcome.

# Details

One aim of this collection is to better organize an extremely complicated
phenomenon without overwhelming. This section documents the structure used.
Does it perfectly capture the infinite nuances of human experience? Of course not.
Is it an advancement over a dictionary? That is for you to decide.

## Emotions vs Experiences

We purposefully avoid the word "emotion". No one agrees on what "emotions" really
are; they can carry cultural baggage, and they don't begin to cover all the experiences we have. For example, is "curiosity" an emotion? How about "introspection"?

The academic study of emotions seems to fall into three camps:

1. A search for the "basic emotions". There's a hypothesis that complex emotions may be composed of a basic set like fear, joy, disgust, love, etc.
1. A focus on the neurobiological aspects of experience like brain structure and neurotransmitters.
1. The view that emotions are "constructed" and culturally dependent.

All of this work is useful and illuminating. We would welcome professional
emotion researchers to comment on the entries for their area of specialty.
We will certainly consider linking to well-established scientific resources.

## Core Philosophies

It took hundreds of thousands of years for our species to figure out how to represent spoken language as marks pressed in clay or scratched onto bones. It took longer still for music to be written down. Everyone understands that these abstract notations are directional – only useful once the amazing brain translates the marks to something you've experienced. 
Each JSON file here describes an experience proposed as being analgous a single 'letter' in the complex tapestry of our inner world. These units can be combined with others to form 'words', which are then strung together to form 'sentences' and ultimately the crazy rhyming autobiography we live inside.
For example, you can probably imagine how it might be possible to feel "fear/anger/guilt" that dissipates into "admiration/humor/relief/shame".

Put another way, it seems as though there are an infinite number of unique experiences because we can feel several at once from a palette of hundreds that form a kaleidoscopic time series.

Here are some other guiding assumptions:

- We must incorporate established science where possible, including psychology, neuroscience, primatology, emotion research, meditation research, philosophy, etc.
- Our inside experiences are related to neurotransmitter chemicals in the brain, but they are not the same.
- Humans are profoundly social creatures with complex mental models of social interactions. A given experience feels very different from the inside than when observed in someone else. For example, your own 'contempt' feels different from perceiving 'contempt' in someone else.
- One of society's functions is to decide which experiences to encourage and which to discourage. This collection needs to include experiences that most people consider harmful or socially inappropriate.
- It's okay to suggest a model for understanding various experiences, but don't assume it's the only one. Allow for multiple underlying explanations as long as they're coherent and illuminating.
- We need to deal carefully with mental health problems. This collection is not a self-help replacement for the DSM and professional therapy.

## Experiences from Different Angles

Distinctions between some experiences can be subtle, so these files try to shed light on the topic in several ways:

- Description - defines the experience and its sources
- Quote - what someone might say who's having this experience
- Examples - situations where this experience may happen
- Variations - many experiences have different flavors
- Comparisons - highlight differences and similarities of closely related experiences
- Commentaries - insightful quotations chosen to highlight various aspects of the experience
- Spectrum - some experiences have different words for varying strength of feeling.
- Model - A section reserved for a technical description of the experience.
